Every fal.ai generation. Model, prompt intent, seed, cost, output path, kept or discarded.
**Index plus the cross-cutting entries; the per-gate logs are in [`docs/generations/`](generations/).**

## The logging contract

🔴 **Every future entry MUST carry its `request_id`.** `npm run assets:fetch`
([ASSET-PIPELINE.md](ASSET-PIPELINE.md) §10) re-fetches job records by request id from a fresh clone
where `_generated/` is absent — and **this log is the only place those ids can live**, because the
job records themselves are gitignored. An entry without a request id is an asset that cannot be
rebuilt. *(4.15, 4.17)* Since the 2026-08-09 split the ids live in the per-gate files under
[`generations/`](generations/); this file carries the contract, the spend and the pointers to them.

**Required columns from Gate 7 onward:** `request_id` · endpoint · seed · every non-default input ·
**quoted** cost · **invoiced** cost once known · output path · kept/discarded · measured dimensions.

⚠️ **The 21 entries in [generations/phase-00-style-probe.md](generations/phase-00-style-probe.md)
predate this contract and have no recorded request ids.** They are style probes, not shipped assets,
so nothing in the game depends on rebuilding them — but they are consequently **not rebuildable**,
and that is stated here rather than discovered later.

## Spend — RECONCILED against the invoice, 2026-08-09

| | clips |
|---|---|
| before this session | 10 |
| this session (V.1 + V.2 + the airborne re-shoot) | 12 |
| **total Seedance clips** | **22** |

Plus one `nano-banana-pro/edit` (V.0) and the earlier image batch.

**The user read the fal.ai dashboard. Phase 4 cost `$31.39` against a `$25` ceiling — `$6.39` over,
a 25.6 % overrun.** The estimate was **$6.21 – $31.60**; the actual landed 0.7 % under the top of
that range.

**The ~22× disagreement is settled, and the pessimistic source was right.**

| source | quoted per clip | 22 clips | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `genmedia pricing` — `0.014 / "units"`, unit undefined | $0.056 | $1.23 | **wrong by ~21×** |
| the dashboard-derived rate | $1.21 | $26.62 | **right to ~1 %** |

Backing out the images at their quoted rates (21 × $0.08 + 23 × $0.15 ≈ $5.13) leaves **≈ $26.26 for
22 clips ≈ $1.19 each** against the $1.21 predicted. That decomposition assumes the *image* quotes
were accurate, which is not independently verified — but the conclusion does not depend on it: even
crediting the images at zero, the clips cost ≥ $1.43 each, still **25× the `genmedia pricing`
figure**. There is no arithmetic that rescues the low estimate.

> 🔴 **`genmedia pricing` is not a price for this endpoint. Do not build a projection on it.**
> It reports a unit it does not define, and on the one endpoint where an invoice now exists it is
> wrong by more than an order of magnitude — in the dangerous direction, cheap. This is
> *(vault 4.9)* recurring on a different CLI: last project's preflight under-reported by ~6×, this
> one by ~21×. **The rule that generalises: when two sources disagree on cost, budget the
> pessimistic one and treat the optimistic one as absent.** Had that rule been applied here, the
> ceiling would have been hit at clip 20 and the overrun would have been caught before it happened
> rather than after.

**Where the money actually went, because the overrun has a cause and it is not the rate.** Five clips
ship. **Twenty-two were paid for**, so ~77 % of the video spend — roughly **$20** — was rework:

| cause of rework | clips | fixed? | gated? |
|---|---|---|---|
| the per-frame packer flattening every animation's vertical motion | ~9 | ✅ yes | ✅ 4.19 / 4.20, mutation-tested |
| the anchor image's own boots 58 px out of level | ~6 | ✅ yes, at source (STYLE.md §8) | ❌ **no — 4.27 is FAIL** |
| the monotonicity clause that made `fall` somersault | ~2 | ✅ yes | ⚠️ prompt-only |

So the expectation that **the next character is far cheaper is well-founded** — a first-pass run is
5 clips plus a probe, ≈ $7 of video against ≈ $26 spent here — **but it is not yet guaranteed.** The
single most expensive defect is the one whose gate is still missing: nothing measures an anchor's
contact geometry before generating from it, which is exactly what criterion **4.27** demands and why
it is recorded FAIL rather than waved through. **Closing 4.27 is the cheapest money this project can
save**; it is worth about six clips.

Five paid generations remain **unattributable** — the orphaned job records above — so they are inside
the $31.39 but cannot be traced to a prompt or a date. That is the practical cost of the vault 4.17
violation, now denominated: **~$6 of spend with no provenance.**

## Session 6 — the padded round

**7 clips, $8.33. Spend $23.51 → $31.84 of $40.** Full provenance, every `request_id`, the
hash-verified anchor uploads and the per-clip verdicts:
[generations/phase-05-padded-round.md](generations/phase-05-padded-round.md).

The headline is not the framing (which is solved — no subject crop in five of seven strips, on two
anchors never ratio-matched before). It is that **padding changes the subject's size in the output,
and `scale` is stored per SLUG**, so the padded courier `attack` packed at 114 px against `hurt`'s
288 px. Padding is a property of a GENERATION and so is the scale it implies. Recorded there in full.

> 🔵 **SUPERSEDED 2026-08-12 (session 7, user decision D2).** The 114px arithmetic above is
> unchanged and correct — what changed is the premise that a per-slug scale had to hold it. Scale
> now resolves per `(slug, action)` (`tools/gen/build-assets.mjs`, `tools/gen/catalogWrite.mjs`), so
> a padded generation can declare its own number instead of sharing the slug default. The padded
> `brass-courier/attack`/`death` rounds this session bought are adopted after all — the unpadded
> re-shoot this session's decision commissioned did not fix the crop (`L188 R0`, unmoved across
> three prompt clauses) — and `attack` now ships at 289px. Full account:
> [generations/phase-05-padded-round.md](generations/phase-05-padded-round.md)'s own dated
> supersession entry.
